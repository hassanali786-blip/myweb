1. Sole Proprietorship
📌 Definition: A Sole Proprietorship is the simplest and oldest form of business organization. It is owned, managed, and controlled by a single individual. The owner provides all the capital, enjoys all the profits, and bears all the risks alone.

👍 Advantages

  • Full Control: Owner has complete decision-making authority without requiring outside approvals.
  • Easy to Start/Dissolve: Minimal legal formalities and low setup/closing costs.
  • Direct Tax Benefits: Profits are taxed directly as personal income, avoiding double taxation.
  • Secrecy: Business trade secrets and financial records remain confidential.
  • Direct Motivation: Receiving 100% of profits incentivizes hard work.

👎 Disadvantages

  • Unlimited Liability: Personal assets can be sold to settle business liabilities and debts.
  • Limited Capital: Fund raising relies solely on personal savings or personal loans.
  • Limited Growth: Expansion is restricted by limited financial and managerial capacity.
  • Lack of Continuity: Business may abruptly end if the owner dies or becomes incapacitated.
2. Partnership
📌 Definition: A Partnership is a business structure where two or more individuals (up to 20) agree to pool their resources, share profits, and jointly manage a business. It is governed by a Partnership Deed which outlines the rights and duties of each partner.

👍 Advantages

  • More Capital: Raises significantly more funds than a sole proprietorship by combining resources.
  • Shared Responsibility: Management duties and workload are divided based on skills.
  • Better Decision Making: Diverse skills, perspectives, and expertise lead to better choices.
  • Shared Risk: Financial losses are shared among all partners, reducing individual burden.

👎 Disadvantages

  • Potential Conflicts: Differences of opinion or disagreements can cause operational issues.
  • Unlimited Liability: Partners face unlimited personal liability for debts incurred by the firm.
  • Limited Life: Firm dissolves if a partner dies, leaves, or becomes bankrupt.
  • Slow Decision Making: Requires consultation and consensus, which can delay actions.
